Internal Sales Prediction Market - Plan#

Mode: /plan. Synthesis of CONTEXT_prediction_markets.txt into a deployable design, TDD, architecture, model-orchestration strategy, and adversarial-review prompt. MVP target: 2 weeks, local-first React/Vite, Supabase + Vercel for hosted phase.

---

1. Executive Summary#

What it is. An internal, play-money prediction market layered on top of Salesforce that lets sales reps, managers, SEs, CSMs, finance, and legal trade contracts on the outcomes of pipeline deals — close-won by date, slip risk, stage transitions, and milestone events. Market prices are published back to the CRM as an independent, crowd-derived probability that sits alongside the rep forecast and any model-driven score (e.g., Einstein, Clari).

Why it works. The corporate-PM literature (HP, Intel, Ford, Google) shows that markets reliably surface latent information that hierarchical forecasting hides — optimism bias, sandbagging, champion risk, procurement reality. Ford's vehicle-sales market beat experts by ~25% on MSE; the simulated DGP in the context file shows market prices absorb the CRM probability in a joint logistic regression (LR test χ² = 4.84, p ≈ 0.028 at n=30). This is decision support, not entertainment.

Design stance. Kalshi-style contract clarity (objective settlement from CRM/finance fields), Polymarket-style distribution (embedded in Slack and Salesforce, fast trades). LMSR automated market maker for liquidity at low participation. Reputation-weighted, role-scoped markets to reward calibration over volume. Play money + recognition + perks. No real-money wagering — sidesteps gambling/HR risk and ends the "betting on coworkers" framing.

The one thing that makes or breaks it. AE dominance of price discovery. If the deal owner can move price, the market just recapitulates the CRM number. Mitigated by position limits per role, AE non-trading on own deals (or limited side only), and reputation weighting from prior calibration.

MVP in 2 weeks. 5 hardcoded demo deals + 100 synthetic Salesforce deals, LMSR market on each, role-scoped trading, Brier-based leaderboard, single-page React/Vite dashboard, local Postgres → Supabase, manual settlement. No Slack/Salesforce write-back in MVP.

2. Product Description (synthesized)#

The Internal Sales Prediction Market ("Prism" working name) is a decision-support layer for revenue organizations. For every qualifying open opportunity, the system instantiates one or more prediction contracts that pay out 1 if a clearly-defined event occurs (deal closes-won by close date; deal slips past quarter; stage advances by Friday) and 0 otherwise. Eligible employees trade those contracts using a play-money budget; an LMSR automated market maker provides continuous liquidity even with thin participation. The last-trade price is interpreted as the crowd's probability and is rendered in three places:

  1. The market's own dashboard (rep, manager, leadership views).
  2. A Salesforce custom field on the Opportunity (Prism_Probability__c) and a sidebar component on the Opp page.
  3. A Slack digest in the deal channel, posted on price-move thresholds and on stage changes.

The product is explicitly framed as "collective deal intelligence," not as betting. The output is a three-layer forecast view — rep forecast, manager forecast, market forecast — and the operating-rhythm payoff is that forecast calls move from "what do you think?" to "why is the market pricing this deal down?"

Primary forecast targets. Close-won-by-date, win/loss, slip risk, deal-confidence band, and event milestones (economic-buyer attendance, redlines arrival, stage transition, discount > 10%, mutual-action-plan completion).

Settlement. Resolved deterministically from canonical Salesforce fields (StageName, IsClosed, IsWon, CloseDate) plus a small set of Prism-managed event flags written by deal owners or managers and dual-confirmed.

3. Use Cases - coherent summary#

The market becomes valuable when it is wired into existing operating rhythms, not bolted on as a side product. Five tightly-coupled use cases:

  1. Pre-forecast-call deal triage. On Monday, RevOps pulls the deals where market price diverges from the rep's stage-implied probability by more than 25 percentage points. Those deals get the 30 minutes of airtime; the rest are accepted on autopilot. The market replaces the "stare at every deal" forecast call.
  1. Slip-risk early warning. A binary market on "Will this deal slip past quarter-end?" tightens 10–14 days before quarter close as SEs, finance, and legal trade on what they're seeing. Spikes in slip probability page the deal owner and the regional VP.
  1. Sandbagging / overconfidence detection. Market lower than rep + manager forecast by a large margin → leadership review for hidden risk. Market higher than internal forecast → potential under-recognized momentum (executive sponsorship, signature pending) the rep hasn't logged.
  1. Event-milestone decomposition. Instead of one binary on close-won, a deal has 4–8 micro-markets on next-step events. The product of those micro-probabilities (with a calibration adjustment) yields a more predictive composite than the close-won market alone, and produces a structured trail of why the deal is moving.
  1. Cross-functional intelligence capture. SEs price technical-validation risk; legal prices contract friction; finance prices pricing/discount risk; marketing prices intent. Role-scoped markets let each function trade where they have edge, and reputation weighting compounds that into who is good at predicting what.

4. Detailed Design#

4.1 Market design#

Contract types (MVP).

ContractResolution eventSettlement sourceWindow
CLOSE_WON_BY_DATEIsWon == true AND CloseDate <= targetSFDC nativeOpens at Stage 2; closes 24h before CloseDate
SLIP_PAST_QUARTERCloseDate rolls past quarter end OR not closed by quarter endSFDC native + cronOpens 30d before QE; closes at QE
STAGE_ADVANCE_BYStageName reaches target stage by dateSFDC nativeOpens on stage entry; closes at target date
MILESTONE_EVENTBoolean flag set by deal owner + manager confirmationPrism event ledgerDefined per deal

Mechanism. Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule (LMSR) automated market maker. Cost function C(q) = b · ln(Σ exp(q_i / b)); price p_i = exp(q_i / b) / Σ exp(q_j / b). b is the liquidity parameter — bigger b = more liquidity, more capital at risk for the market maker. Pick b per market based on (a) expected number of traders and (b) maximum acceptable subsidy if the market resolves wrong-way. Default: b = 50 for deal-level markets, b = 20 for milestone markets.

Why LMSR over order book. With <50 traders per market, an order book has terrible liquidity — bid-ask spreads are wide, prices stale. LMSR provides continuous prices, bounds the market maker's loss, and is the standard in corporate PM literature (HP, Intel used scoring-rule mechanisms).

Settlement. A resolver job runs nightly and on-demand at quarter-end. For SFDC-native contracts, the resolver reads canonical fields and pays out 1 or 0. For milestone contracts, the deal owner submits the event flag and a second party (manager, SE, or RevOps) confirms; the audit trail is immutable.

Partial information / slippage / deal updates.

  • Stage change while a market is open: market continues; the new stage is data the crowd can trade on.
  • CloseDate change: the original CLOSE_WON_BY_DATE contract resolves on the original date (otherwise the rep can move the goalposts); a new market can be opened for the new date.
  • Deal merged / split: original markets resolve based on the surviving record's outcome, with manual tie-breaking by RevOps logged in the audit.
  • Deal closed-lost early: market resolves immediately, no waiting for the date.

4.2 Participant model#

Leadership trading is forbidden — it would create reverse pressure and make the price about reading the boss's mind. Leadership consumes the signal; they don't produce it.

AEs trade their own deals freely, but every trade and standing position is public — visible on the market page, in the manager dashboard, and in the deal-channel digest. Transparency, not prohibition, is the anti-sandbagging mechanism: if the AE shorts their own deal, the manager sees it the same minute the trade lands. This preserves the AE's first-party signal — the central reason markets beat hierarchies in the HP/Intel/Ford literature — while making manipulation expensive in social capital. The new diagnostic this unlocks is "rep forecast vs. rep position" divergence: a rep saying 90% but buying only 5 YES at 0.40 and not adding when price drops is acting against their own stated forecast — itself high-signal. That divergence becomes a column in the manager dashboard.

Reputation weight is applied to a parallel calibrated price that leadership sees in addition to the raw market price; raw price still drives payouts.

4.3 Incentive system#

Currency. Play money ("Prism Points") allocated quarterly: 1,000 points per active participant, plus performance allocation based on prior-quarter Brier score. No real-money wagering — clears legal/HR/compliance and ends the framing problem.

Scoring. Two leaderboards, both visible:

  1. Profit/loss in points (raw market outcome).
  2. Brier score on positions held at market close: BS = (1/N) Σ (p_i − o_i)². Lower is better.

The advancement leaderboard for perks/recognition uses a calibration-adjusted score: S = α · normalized_PnL + (1 − α) · (1 − BS) with α = 0.4. This explicitly rewards being well-calibrated (40% prediction at 38% realized rate scores well) instead of just picking winners — that is the core anti-gamification lever.

Perks. Quarterly: top decile gets recognition + an extra 500-point allocation; team-level Brier wins funds team-discretionary budget. No individual cash payouts.

Anti-manipulation.

  • Position limits per role per market (table above) make it expensive to push price.
  • No anonymous trades in scope-relevant markets — internal markets, all trades attributed.
  • AE position transparency on own deals — every trade public, every standing position visible in the manager dashboard. Sandbagging is exposed, not blocked.
  • Wash-trade detection: same user round-tripping in <60s is flagged and the trades zeroed.
  • Information rights: notes attached to markets can move trader belief but only post deal-owner verification, preventing rumor-driven swings.
  • Reputation decay: a participant who has traded on <3 markets in 90 days has their weight reset to 1.0×.

4.4 Market rules#

  • Trading windows. Open at Stage 2 (Qualified Pipeline) entry, close 24h before CloseDate for close-won markets, at QE for slip markets, at target date for stage/milestone markets. No after-hours close — markets run 24×7 to allow async global trading.
  • Position limits. As in 4.2.
  • Suspension rules. Auto-suspend trading on (a) deal-stage change, for 2 hours, to let everyone read the new stage; (b) deal-owner-flagged "material change" event (acquisition, RFP issued, champion left); (c) RevOps-initiated investigation. Suspensions logged.
  • Stage / forecast resets. Stage reverts (Stage 4 → Stage 3) re-open the market for trading but do not unwind prior trades. CloseDate moves do not move existing contracts (see 4.1).
  • Anti-gaming. As in 4.3, plus: a "cool-off" prohibition on trading in markets you have written the deal-owner notes for in the last 24h. A conflict-of-interest flag for finance/legal traders on deals where they are the assigned reviewer — disclosed but not blocked.

4.5 Data integration#

Canonical fields (read from Salesforce). Opportunity.Id, AccountId, Owner.Id, Amount, CloseDate, StageName, ForecastCategory, IsClosed, IsWon, Probability (CRM), CreatedDate, LastModifiedDate, LastActivityDate. Plus custom: Prism_Probability__c (write-back), Prism_Last_Sync__c.

Read-from / write-back boundary. Prism reads SFDC opportunity state; Prism writes Prism_Probability__c and Prism_Last_Sync__c only. Never mutates standard SFDC fields.

Signal feeds (MVP+1, not in 2-week MVP).

  • Activity / engagement (Outreach, Salesloft, Gong) → market context panel only, not auto-traded.
  • Intent (6sense, Bombora) → context.
  • Email events (open/reply rates) → context.

Reconciliation. SFDC Opportunity.Id is the system-of-record key. Prism stores its own internal_deal_id for routing/UX but always re-anchors to sfdc_opportunity_id on every sync.

Signal-quality checks.

  • Stage-vs-CloseDate sanity: a Stage-2 deal with a CloseDate in 5 days is flagged.
  • Stale opportunities: no activity in 30+ days → market suspended pending owner confirmation.
  • Duplicate detection: same Account + similar Amount + nearby CloseDate → flagged for RevOps merge.
  • CloseDate-in-the-past: blocked from market creation.

4.6 User experience#

Trading interface (per market).

  • Top: deal name, account, amount, stage, owner, close date, days-to-close.
  • Center: price chart (last 30 days), current YES/NO price, last trade.
  • Right: trade panel — buy YES / buy NO, slider for amount, computed cost via LMSR cost function, projected payoff, calibration warning if user's prior Brier < median.
  • Below: deal context panel — recent stage history, recent activities, attached deal-owner notes.
  • Bottom: open positions (yours), market history, dispute / flag button.

Speed. Sub-1-second trade execution; sub-200ms price refresh on open market. Optimistic UI.

Forecast-meeting view (manager / leadership).

  • Three-column table: deals × (rep forecast %, manager forecast %, market price %, divergence flag).
  • Sort by absolute divergence — top 10 are the agenda.
  • Click-through to per-deal market with the full context panel.

Slack integration (post-MVP). Daily digest in #forecast-{region}: "5 deals where market diverges >25pts from rep forecast." Per-deal channel pings on price moves >10pts intraday.

4.7 Operating model#

  • Owner. A single GTM Engineer owns Prism end-to-end — sits between RevOps and Engineering so they can both ship code and run the market. Responsibilities: opening markets, suspending on disputes, settling milestone contracts, weekly leaderboard publication, quarterly performance review, and the steering-committee report-out. The GTM Engineer is the named accountable individual; without that name the operating model is decorative.
  • Cadence. Markets open continuously as deals reach Stage 2; close per 4.4. Quarterly: review of market-vs-actual accuracy, calibration plots, leaderboard distribution, any manipulation incidents. Annual: design review (mechanism, incentive structure, perks).
  • Governance. A small steering committee — RevOps, Sales Leadership, Legal, HR — meets quarterly, owns design changes. All design changes require steering committee approval; all incident dispositions are logged and visible to participants.
  • Performance review. Three measures, quarterly:

- Forecast accuracy: market Brier vs. rep Brier vs. manager Brier vs. CRM-stage-implied Brier. - Incremental information: logistic regression LR test (CRM + market vs. CRM only) — exactly the test in the context file. - Behavioral: count of deal-review interventions triggered by market divergence, and their downstream win/loss.

Component responsibilities.

  • Client. React 18 + Vite + TypeScript + TanStack Query + Tailwind + shadcn/ui. Supabase Realtime subscription for price ticks. Recharts or visx for price/calibration charts.
  • API layer. Next.js (App Router) deployed on Vercel; locally pnpm dev. Stateless routes, hit Supabase Postgres for state, AMM core for pricing math, LLM agents for context synthesis.
  • Postgres (Supabase). Authoritative state — markets, positions, trades, ledger, users, reputation, settlements. Row-level security keyed by auth.uid(). The trade endpoint runs all writes inside one transaction with SERIALIZABLE isolation to prevent race conditions on the AMM q vector.
  • AMM core. Pure TypeScript module (packages/amm). LMSR cost & price functions. No I/O. Imported by the trade endpoint and by the resolver. Unit-tested to numerical tolerance against Hanson's original LMSR formulation.
  • Resolver. Vercel cron job, hourly + on-demand. Pulls SFDC opportunities, detects resolved markets, executes payouts inside a single transaction, writes back Prism_Probability__c and the settlement log.
  • LLM agents. Anthropic API. Three roles (see §7): Haiku for context summarization on each market open, Sonnet for code-gen and design-doc work, Opus for strategic / statistical / adversarial review.
  • Event ledger. Append-only events table — every trade, settlement, suspension, admin action. Source of truth for audit and replay.
  • Salesforce sandbox. A Developer-Edition org seeded with 100 synthetic deals (see §6.7) plus 5 hardcoded demo deals. JSForce client for reads; Bulk API 2.0 for the 100-deal load.

Suggested additional technical assets (beyond the context file's list):

  • Inngest or Trigger.dev for durable background jobs (resolver, sync, LLM batched calls). Vercel cron is fine for MVP; durable runners matter when LLM batches or SFDC bulk loads need retry.
  • PostHog for product analytics + feature flags (used to gate role-scoped markets in early rollout).
  • Sentry for error tracking — non-negotiable since money flows through the AMM.
  • Vitest + Playwright for unit + E2E test runners.
  • Zod for schema validation at API boundaries.
  • Drizzle ORM over Supabase Postgres — typed migrations, no raw SQL drift.
  • Resend for transactional email (settlement notifications).
  • Slack Bolt SDK (post-MVP) for deal-channel digests and trade commands.

6.4 Core logic flows#

Trade flow (the only complex path):

client clicks "Buy 10 YES"
  → POST /api/trades
    → BEGIN TRANSACTION (SERIALIZABLE)
      → SELECT market FOR UPDATE
      → check market.status == 'OPEN'
      → check position limits for user role
      → check AE-not-shorting-own-deal rule
      → cost = lmsrCost(qYes + 10, qNo, b) − lmsrCost(qYes, qNo, b)
      → check user.points_balance >= cost
      → UPDATE markets SET q_yes = q_yes + 10
      → INSERT trades (...)
      → UPSERT positions (...)
      → UPDATE users SET points_balance = points_balance − cost
      → INSERT events (type='TRADE', payload={...})
    → COMMIT
  → publish on Supabase Realtime channel `market:{id}`
  → return { trade, newPrice, position }

If SERIALIZABLE retries because of a concurrent trade, the API retries up to 3× before returning a 409 to the client. The AMM math is deterministic — the retry is safe.

Settlement flow. Cron at top of every hour. For each market where resolution_date <= now():

  1. Fetch latest SFDC state for the parent deal.
  2. Apply contract resolution logic (close-won? slipped? stage advanced?).
  3. For each position in that market: payout = yesShares × outcome + noShares × (1 − outcome). Credit user.
  4. Record Brier contribution = (price_at_close − outcome)² per held share.
  5. Mark market RESOLVED; write Prism_Probability__c historical.
  6. Append events.

6.5 Statistical / ML / mathematical models - in depth#

6.5.1 LMSR (the AMM)#

Cost function over outcomes q = (q_yes, q_no):

C(q) = b · ln(exp(q_yes / b) + exp(q_no / b))

Price (instantaneous):

p_yes = exp(q_yes / b) / (exp(q_yes / b) + exp(q_no / b))
p_no  = 1 − p_yes

Cost of buying Δ YES shares: C(q_yes + Δ, q_no) − C(q_yes, q_no).

Maximum subsidy (worst-case loss to market maker if all probability resolves on one side): b · ln(2). Choose b = 50 → max subsidy 34.7 points per market. With 100 active markets, max system subsidy is ≈ 3,470 points — comfortably within the play-money budget.

6.5.2 Brier score & calibration#

For each user u, period T:

BS_u = (1 / N_T) · Σ_{trades held to close} (p_at_close − outcome)²

Calibration: bin trades by predicted probability deciles, plot realized rate per bin. Reliability diagram + ECE (expected calibration error) reported per quarter.

6.5.3 Hypothesis test (LR test on incremental information - directly from the context file)#

Fit two logistic regressions on resolved deals:

  • Model A: outcome ~ logit(crm_probability)
  • Model C: outcome ~ logit(crm_probability) + logit(market_probability_at_close)

LR statistic: 2 · (logL_C − logL_A) ~ χ²(1). Reject H₀ at p < 0.05 → market carries incremental information. This is the single quantitative metric that justifies the product to leadership.

Implemented in packages/stats via mlr-glm or directly with glm-js / a small Newton-Raphson logistic fit. Power analysis: at α = 0.05, power 0.8, expected effect size from the simulated DGP, n ≈ 80–120 resolved deals is the minimum for a credible test. Below that, report directionally only.

6.5.4 Reputation weighting (Bayesian)#

For each user, model their forecasting skill as:

skill_u ~ Beta(α_u, β_u)

Update from realized Brier per trade: a held trade with (p, outcome) contributes a likelihood proportional to a Bernoulli with parameter p. Posterior mean of skill → reputation weight, clamped to [0.5, 2.0].

Calibrated price (leadership view):

p_calibrated = Σ_u (w_u · trade_size_u · price_u) / Σ_u (w_u · trade_size_u)

i.e., a weighted average of trades, weighted by trader reputation × trade size. Raw price still drives payouts; calibrated price is informational only.

6.5.5 Composite probability from milestone markets#

For a deal with milestone markets m_1 ... m_k that are individually predictive of close-won, fit a logistic regression on resolved deals:

P(close_won) = σ(β_0 + Σ β_i · logit(p_i))

Refit quarterly. The composite is a better predictor than any single market when milestones are conditionally informative; it also produces SHAP-style attributions (which milestone is moving the deal).

8. Risks & Failure Modes#

  1. AE price-discovery dominance. AE knows their deal best; if uncapped, market = AE's hunch. Mitigated by position limits + AE-no-short rule, but verify in pilot.
  2. Sandbagging. Rep talks down their deal in market to overperform later. Addressed by AE position transparency (every AE trade on own deal is public), plus the rep-forecast-vs-rep-position divergence diagnostic in the manager dashboard. Residual risk: a manager or peer-AE shorts on the rep's behalf. Detection: monitor for clusters of NO trades from accounts socially adjacent to the rep, and compare market trajectory vs. rep-forecast trajectory for persistent gaps.
  3. Insider info / fairness. Finance trader sees a discount memo before it's logged. Mitigation: scope-relevant restrictions, conflict-of-interest disclosures, and audit logs; legal/HR review of incidents.
  4. Compliance / "betting" framing. Even with play money, leadership may pattern-match to gambling. Frame relentlessly as "collective deal intelligence" and avoid gambling-adjacent UX (no "wagers", "odds in betting form" — use probabilities and confidence).
  5. Liquidity collapse. Below ~10 trades/market, LMSR price is dominated by the first few traders. Mitigation: subsidize liquidity via larger b; require ≥3 distinct traders before publishing market price to managers.
  6. Goodhart's law on Brier. Once Brier drives perks, traders may avoid markets where they can't predict accurately, lowering participation breadth. Mitigation: reward participation breadth in addition to accuracy; calibration-adjusted score.
  7. Salesforce data drift. SFDC fields rename / change semantics. Mitigation: nightly schema validation; a single sync module; treat field changes as breaking.
  8. AMM bugs. A signed-shares bug can drain the points budget instantly. Mitigation: property-based tests on packages/amm against Hanson's reference; per-trade invariant checks (p_yes + p_no = 1 ± 1e-6).
  9. Cultural side-effects. Public leaderboards can demoralize. Mitigation: leaderboards by team, not individual, in early rollout; opt-in for individual public rank.
  10. Discontinuation risk. PMs at Google ended when key sponsors left. Mitigation: get explicit RevOps + Sales-Leadership ownership signed in operating model; report quarterly metrics so the value is legible.

9. Phased Rollout#

Phase 0 — MVP (Weeks 1–2). Local React/Vite + Postgres + LMSR + 5 hardcoded deals + 100 synthetic deals (no live SFDC). 5 internal users (engineer, RevOps lead, 1 AE, 1 manager, 1 SE). Goal: prove trading flow + price formation.

Phase 1 — Closed Pilot (Weeks 3–6). Supabase + Vercel deploy. Connect real SFDC sandbox. One sales region, ~20 participants, ~30 active deals. Run actual quarterly cycle, settle, compute Brier + LR test (will be underpowered — that's OK). Iterate on UX from forecast-meeting feedback.

Phase 2 — Full Pilot (Weeks 7–14). One full sales segment, ≥100 participants, ≥150 deals, full quarter. Slack integration. Salesforce sidebar. First quarterly leaderboard + perks. This is the phase where the LR-test result becomes credible.

Phase 3 — Production (Quarter 2+). Org-wide. Steering committee operating quarterly. Milestone markets enabled. Reputation weighting active. Calibrated-price view in leadership dashboard.

12. Locked Decisions#

  1. AE trading on own deals: yes, both sides, with public positions. Transparency replaces prohibition. AE first-party signal is preserved; sandbagging is exposed in real time. Adds a new diagnostic — rep-forecast vs. rep-position divergence — to the manager dashboard.
  2. Currency: play money + perks. No real money. Sidesteps gambling/HR/legal posture; clears the cultural framing problem.
  3. SFDC sandbox: available. MVP loads the 100 synthetic deals + 5 demo deals into the sandbox via Bulk API 2.0; Prism reads through JSForce on a 5-minute sync cadence.

  5. Pilot population: 10-person synthetic sales team. Controlled demo population; explicitly not statistically powered.

Implications worth flagging#

Liquidity at n=10. Ten traders × ~30 active markets is thin. Compensate by (a) raising LMSR b to 80–100 to keep prices responsive, (b) capping active markets at ~25 instead of all 100, (c) reporting calibration plots and trade-trajectory case studies in lieu of a significance test. The §6.5.3 LR test belongs to Phase 2 — at n=10 traders and however many deals resolve in the demo window, the test will be underpowered to the point of being meaningless. Don't claim it.

AE position transparency requires UX support. Add to the trade panel: a "your trades on this deal will be public" notice for the deal owner. Add to the manager dashboard: a column showing the AE's net position (in shares and cost basis) on each of their deals, alongside their stated forecast.
