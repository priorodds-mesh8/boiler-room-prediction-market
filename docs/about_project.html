Big Picture

Plain-English framing for the product.
Core narrative

Boiler Room is a sales prediction game and dashboard. Imagine your company has a bunch of possible sales deals. Each deal asks a simple question:

Will this customer buy before the deadline?

Boiler Room turns those sales questions into little markets where the answer is basically YES or NO. Instead of only trusting one sales rep's guess, Boiler Room compares three kinds of forecasts.

1. ML model

The model's guess

The machine learning pipeline looks at deal data and estimates a probability, like a 62% chance of closing.

2. Market

The crowd or agent price

Agents or people buy and sell YES/NO positions. If YES costs 70 cents, the market is saying about 70% chance.

3. You

The human player's call

In a Practice Run, you review deal information, then forecast whether each deal will close or skip the day.

Who made the best probability forecast: ML, the market, or You?

P&L matters, but the real winner is whoever guessed closest to reality.

Corporate Precedent

Prediction markets are not just a toy model; companies have used them to forecast operational outcomes.
Research-backed

The theory behind prediction markets is that useful knowledge inside an organization is often distributed, local, and politically awkward to say out loud. A market gives people a low-friction way to reveal that knowledge through prices instead of meetings. When someone buys YES, sells NO, or refuses to trade at a posted price, they are turning private signal into a public probability. The resulting price becomes a compact, continuously updated forecast that can surface latent information from sales reps, operators, product teams, finance, legal, and support before it appears in official plans.

Google

Internal markets for company events

Google ran a large internal prediction-market program, including markets about product launches, office openings, and other strategically relevant events. Research on the program found useful forecasts, but also documented biases such as optimism and showed that trading patterns revealed information flow inside the company.

Sources: Columbia Business School, Corporate Prediction Markets study

Ford and Firm X

Markets compared with expert forecasts

Bo Cowgill and Eric Zitzewitz studied corporate prediction markets at Google, Ford Motor Company, and an anonymous basic-materials conglomerate called Firm X. Even with thin participation and weaker incentives than public markets, the markets were relatively efficient and improved on expert forecasts at all three firms, with error reductions reported up to 25%.

Source: Columbia Business School summary

Hewlett Packard

Sales forecasting experiments

HP tested internal markets for sales forecasting in the late 1990s. Summaries of the Chen and Plott work report that market predictions were closer than official forecasts in six of eight comparable events, and that prices produced probability distributions consistent with actual outcomes.

Sources: O'Leary review, HP setup summary

Microsoft and Best Buy

Internal adoption beyond research labs

Microsoft researchers reported using Hanson's automated market maker to run many markets internally. Best Buy's TagTrade is another documented corporate market, with research noting employee participation and use as a decision-support mechanism.

Sources: Journal of Prediction Markets, Decision Support Systems abstract

Why this matters for Boiler Room Boiler Room applies the same information-aggregation idea to revenue forecasting: sales and deal knowledge is scattered across people, systems, and agents, while market prices turn that scattered knowledge into a comparable probability signal.

Practice Run / Boiler Room

The game-like part of Boiler Room.
Gameplay

You compete against agent sales reps over a selectable 5-, 15-, or 30-day run.

Run length

Day 1 through the selected final day

Runs settle only after the final day.

Deal counts

1, 3, 5, or 10 deals

The game can be a focused read or a broader portfolio challenge.

Daily action

Forecast or skip

You must make a call for every active deal. Confidence from 1 to 5 controls forecast strength.

Every day gives you new information. At settlement, the app reveals actual outcomes and compares ML baseline probability, final market probability, your probability, actual result, forecast winner, and P&L.

How Agents Are Represented

Agents are data-driven right now, not separate code files with individual brains.
Implementation note

Each agent does not currently have its own separate file like agents/ae_17.js, agents/se_4.js, or agents/sales_manager_9.js. Agents are represented as data objects and interpreted through shared logic.

Older local simulation

Hardcoded demo personas

The original local demo keeps personas inside app.js as JavaScript objects. Examples include Nadia Price, Leo Grant, Talia Stone, Casey Vale, Quinn Okafor, and Victoria Sloan.

Boiler Room / Practice Run

Agents come from deal data

In the newer Supabase-backed game, each deal carries account-team members such as AE_17, BDR_4, SE_12, SalesManager_8, Legal_3, and CSM_21. Those names live in the deal payload, not separate files.

Current mental model

Here is a deal. Here are the people on that deal. Use their role to decide how they act.

The game builds a roster in api/_game.js, then calculates how each role thinks using shared logic such as agentBelief(...). The current system does not load AE_17's personal brain from a file.

OpenAI endpoint

Shared decision service

The OpenAI API can produce an agent-style decision through /api/agent-decision, but that is one shared endpoint. It does not store a unique memory or file per agent.

Possible upgrade

Persistent agent profiles

A natural next step would be to give each agent type or named agent its own profile, memory, risk tolerance, blind spots, and trading style.

Simple answer No, each agent does not have its own file yet. Agents are generated from deal/team data and shared role logic.

How It Interacts With The ML Pipeline

The pipeline prepares the data; Boiler Room serves it to users.
Architecture

Kitchen

The ML pipeline creates synthetic sales deals, trains models, exports deal data, and loads it into Supabase.

Restaurant

The Boiler Room app reads the prepared live deal pool and serves the experience to users.

The command in the repo is roughly:

python3 -m synthetic_pipeline.run_full_pipeline --n 10000 --live-n 200
  • Create 10,000 synthetic deals.
  • Train baseline ML models on them.
  • Export a smaller live set, like 200 held-out deals, for the Practice Run.
  • Load those into Supabase.

The app does not show all 10,000 deals at once. Instead, it uses a smaller live deal pool produced by the pipeline.

Where The Data Lives

Supabase tables and visibility rules.
Data model
Table Purpose
agent_sim_live_deals Safe visible deal data, market question, baseline ML probabilities, and agent signal seeds.
synthetic_deal_events Timeline events for deals, like legal movement, procurement changes, buyer sentiment, activity, and related signals.
synthetic_deal_outcomes The hidden answer key. This says what actually happened. The browser does not get this until settlement.
game_sessions Stores each Boiler Room run.
game_session_deals Stores which deals were assigned to the run.
game_turns Stores daily intel cards and summaries.
game_actions Stores You and agent actions.
Important security rule Hidden outcomes stay server-side. The browser is not allowed to peek.

How A Boiler Room Run Works

A day-by-day loop from deal selection to settlement.
Flow
1
Day 1 starts. The server picks deals where there is useful disagreement, such as ML at 75%, market at 52%, and mixed agent signals.
2
The app creates intel cards. Cards include Metric, Process, Agent, and Rumor types. Rumors are noisy and clearly labeled as unreliable.
3
You review the cards and deal panels. The player forms a probability judgment from structured context and daily signal changes.
4
You forecast or skip each deal. Forecast will close means YES is underpriced. Forecast won't close means NO is better. Skip means no position change. Confidence from 1 to 5 controls strength.
5
The day ends and the market updates. Agents move too, prices change, and the next day begins until final-day settlement.

The Point Of The App

The practical question Boiler Room is trying to answer.
Why it matters
Can a market plus agent activity plus ML produce a better forecast than normal sales forecasting?

Normal forecasting often depends on rep opinions, manager judgment, and CRM stages. Boiler Room adds model predictions, market pricing, agent behavior, human judgment, and hidden final outcomes.

Over time, the system reveals who is actually good at predicting revenue.
